Title: Scaling and autoscaling
Date: 2026-03-15
Team: Platform
Tags: scaling, hpa, autoscaling, capacity, kubernetes

OrbitOps scales at two levels.

Pods: Horizontal Pod Autoscalers (k8s/30-hpa.yaml) keep the backend between 2 and 5 replicas, adding pods when average CPU passes 60 percent, and the frontend between 2 and 4 replicas at 70 percent. The backend can scale freely because it is stateless: chats, uploads and vectors live in MySQL and Chroma, and rate-limit counters in Redis.

Nodes: the GKE cluster autoscaler keeps between 2 and 4 e2-standard-2 nodes. When pods are Pending because no node has room, it adds a node within a few minutes.

If the Health check reports that the autoscaler is at its maximum, load exceeds the configured capacity. Options: raise maxReplicas in k8s/30-hpa.yaml, raise the node maximum, or check whether one expensive request type (long uploaded documents, research-agent questions) is driving CPU.

Manual scaling for tests or demos: kubectl -n orbitops scale deployment/backend --replicas=4. Note the HPA will move it back within its min/max range.

Data services (MySQL, Chroma, Redis) run as single replicas and are not autoscaled.
